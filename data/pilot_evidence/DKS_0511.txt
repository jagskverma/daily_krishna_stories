# STORY DKS_0511 — Pingala the Courtesan's Renunciation

## Index entry
title: Pingala the Courtesan's Renunciation
summary: The courtesan Pingala, waiting through the night for a client who never comes, is seized with disgust at her own longing, renounces it, and sleeps in peace, teaching that hope is the worst of pains and despair the highest joy.

## Sources and evidence excerpts
- Srimad Bhagavata Purana (Sanskrit, GRETIL) | book/canto 11 | chapter/section 8 | verses 21-43 | confidence 5
  excerpt: Pingala said: 'Look at the extent of my delusion! I sought a lover for gain, when the giver of all joy is ever near. What I sought so wretchedly, let me give up.' Cutting off her hope of a lover, Pingala sat down and slept in peace: 'Hope is the greatest of pains; freedom from hope is the greatest of joys.' (rendered from Sanskrit, verses 30, 42-43)
