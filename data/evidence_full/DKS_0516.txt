## Index entry
title: Pururava's Lament for Urvashi
summary: King Pururava (Aila), mad with grief when the nymph Urvashi leaves him, pursues her naked and weeping; realizing his delusion, he sings the song of Aila on the futility of attachment to women, and attains peace.

## Sources and evidence excerpts
- Srimad Bhagavata Purana (Sanskrit, GRETIL) | book/canto 11 | chapter/section 26 | verses 3-24 | confidence 5
  excerpt: King Aila sang: 'Alas, what delusion has seized me! A monarch, I became a plaything of a woman; I, who was emperor, followed her like a beast, naked and weeping. What is this foul body of flesh and bone, and what these charms imputed to it? So let no wise man cling to women.' (rendered from Sanskrit, verses 6-21)
