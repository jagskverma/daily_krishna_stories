# STORY DKS_0515 — The Miserly Brahmin of Avanti

## Index entry
title: The Miserly Brahmin of Avanti
summary: A wealthy but miserly brahmin of Avanti, spurned by his own relatives and stripped of his wealth, is seized with dispassion, becomes a wandering mendicant, and, enduring the mockery and blows of the crowd, sings the song of the mendicant: the mind alone is the cause of joy and sorrow.

## Sources and evidence excerpts
- Srimad Bhagavata Purana (Sanskrit, GRETIL) | book/canto 11 | chapter/section 23 | verses 5-60 | confidence 5
  excerpt: The brahmin of Avanti, once wealthy, miserly and cruel, saw his wealth taken by relatives and thieves; dispassion arose, and he became a silent, wandering mendicant. Though people seized his staff, mocked and struck him, he sang: 'Neither people, nor gods, nor fate are the cause of my joy and sorrow — it is the mind alone that turns the wheel of existence.' (rendered from Sanskrit, verses 5-11, 41
