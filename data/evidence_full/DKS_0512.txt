## Index entry
title: The Osprey, the Child, the Maiden and the Arrow-Maker
summary: The avadhuta learns from the osprey (letting go of prey seized by stronger birds), the child (freedom from care), the maiden (solitude — she broke her bangles one by one to stop their clinking), and the arrow-maker (one-pointed concentration).

## Sources and evidence excerpts
- Srimad Bhagavata Purana (Sanskrit, GRETIL) | book/canto 11 | chapter/section 9 | verses 1-12 | confidence 5
  excerpt: The avadhuta said: 'A maiden, alone, husking grain, heard her bangles clink and broke them one by one until only one remained, and it was silent — so the sage should dwell alone. And like the arrow-maker so absorbed in straightening his shaft that he did not see the king pass by, the mind should be fixed on one point.' (rendered from Sanskrit, verses 4-12)
